Mezzanine Floors and Platforms

An industrial mezzanine floor is a structural steel platform built inside an existing building to add usable floor area without extending the building. ProRack designs free-standing and rack-supported mezzanines for floor loads from 350 kg/m² to 2,000 kg/m² and more, engineered in Tekla Structures and RSTAB.

How it works

How mezzanine floors and platforms works

Columns and beams carry a new floor at an intermediate height in the building. A free-standing mezzanine has its own steel frame, while a rack-supported mezzanine uses the racking below as its structure.

Stairs, handrails, gates and loading points are designed with the floor. Column positions are coordinated with the slab, the racking below and the material flow.

Is it the right fit?

When to choose mezzanine floors and platforms

  • You need more floor area but cannot extend the building
  • You want offices, packing or assembly above storage
  • The building has clear height above the working floor
  • You want space that can later be moved or extended

Questions

Mezzanine Floors and Platforms FAQs

What floor loads can a ProRack mezzanine carry?

ProRack mezzanine floors are designed for floor loads from 350 kg/m² to 2,000 kg/m² and more. The design load is set from the intended use, such as offices, storage or machinery, and confirmed in the structural design.

What is the difference between a free-standing and a rack-supported mezzanine?

A free-standing mezzanine has its own columns and beams and leaves an open floor below. A rack-supported mezzanine uses the storage racking as its structure, which is efficient when the area below is used for racking.

Do mezzanine floors need approvals?

Requirements vary by state and building use, so check with the local authority and fire consultant early. ProRack prepares the structural design and drawings in Tekla Structures and RSTAB as part of the project.
