Very Narrow-Aisle (VNA) Racking

Very narrow-aisle (VNA) racking is pallet racking with aisles as narrow as about 1.5 m, served by guided turret trucks or man-up order pickers. It can reach building heights of 18 m and more and gives about 40% to 45% higher storage density than a conventional layout.

How it works

How very narrow-aisle (VNA) racking works

Aisles are reduced to little more than the width of the pallet, and specialised turret trucks lift and turn the load inside the aisle. The trucks are guided by a wire in the floor or by rails, so they run safely close to the racking.

Because VNA trucks reach great heights in a narrow aisle, the floor slab must be very flat and the racking, truck and building are designed together.

Is it the right fit?

When to choose very narrow-aisle (VNA) racking

  • Land or building space is expensive
  • The building is tall and you want to use its full height
  • You need access to every pallet as well as high density
  • You are prepared to invest in guided VNA trucks

When another system may fit better

  • Wide-aisle racking if you want to keep standard forklifts.
  • Drive-in racking if you store large quantities of a few SKUs.
  • Mezzanine floors to add floor area for picking or offices.

Questions

Very Narrow-Aisle (VNA) Racking FAQs

How narrow can the aisles be in VNA racking?

VNA aisles can be reduced to about 1.5 m. The exact width depends on the pallet size, the VNA truck model and the guidance system, and is fixed during layout design.

What floor is needed for VNA racking?

VNA trucks lift loads to great heights in narrow aisles, so the floor slab must be very flat and level along the truck wheel tracks. Floor flatness, slab capacity and guidance installation are checked as part of the design.

How much more storage does VNA racking provide?

VNA layouts typically give about 40% to 45% higher storage density than conventional wide-aisle racking on the same footprint, while keeping direct access to every pallet.
